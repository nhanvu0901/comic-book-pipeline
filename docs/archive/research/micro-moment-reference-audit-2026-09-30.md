# Micro-moment scout: reference audit (2026-09-30)

## What was inspected

The local archive `research/competitor_audio_2026-09-28/` contains metadata for
**3,815** Shorts across five requested channels and **3,808** downloaded MP3s.
Seven videos required sign-in. The archive contains **34 SRT transcripts**
selected across high-view, recent, and middle entries. I scanned the full
title/view catalogue and read those 34 transcripts. I did **not** transcribe
all 3,808 recordings. View counts are one public snapshot; the TSVs have no
reliable upload date, retention, viewed-versus-swiped rate, or production
quality measurement. Title patterns and view counts describe the archive; they
cannot isolate why a video performed well. Transcripts can also mistranscribe
names and are leads, not evidence that a comic scene happened.

The top catalogue itself shows why one formula is too narrow: Comic_Escape's
[Wolverine's children](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/AwTMifpoFmE) (23M) is a
list; ComicsUnlocked's [three mutant powers](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/T52AmoEmiGs)
(16M) is another. Those are successful *reference formats*, but they do not
belong in a one-moment scout. Conversely, a concrete action in a title is no
guarantee: Comic_Escape's [Wonder Woman humbles Batman](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/swHZnuVjM14)
had 2.8K in the same snapshot.

## Scout decision rule

1. **One promise:** a familiar subject and one situation intelligible to a
   casual viewer. A second famous name helps but is optional.
2. **One sourced turn:** identify the **specific action, decision, or spoken
   revelation**, its actor, and the earlier expectation it changes. "He was
   tactically patient" or "a battle of truths" is not a scene description.
3. **One direct consequence:** the result belongs to the same scene or narrow
   sequence in one issue. An entire issue recap can supply a candidate only
   when this piece stands alone in 35–50 seconds.
4. **Exact source identity:** series, volume where ambiguous, issue, year,
   comic rather than adaptation, a retrieved source sentence for the decisive
   event and result, and a scrapable reader. Competitor scripts suggest leads;
   independently verify the comic before production.
5. **Rank, do not hard-gate, secondary signals:** recency, famous second
   character, violated constant, visual spectacle, fan discussion, and English
   YouTube coverage. Search both recent and evergreen issues unless a user
   explicitly restricts the year. A fan quote or a large public view count
   never substitutes for beat evidence.

This policy aims to match the **selection grammar** visible in the references.
No scout can promise 100% of their views without the channels' retention,
distribution, age, visuals, and audience data. Test finished Shorts against
Grimframe's own viewed-versus-swiped and retention analytics.
